Here's a very high-level plot synopsis of how I'd imagine at least part of the series playing out:

Issue #1: Jinx recovers Peter from an installation on a marginal research colony, where he has been kept imprisoned with his brain erased. Jinx helps Peter restore his mind using his powers -- a first hint at his abilities to counteract entropy and undo irreversible changes. Peter starts the story disoriented, but happy to be rescued from imprisonment by Jinx. Jinx is focused on the mystery of Earth at first, and encourages Peter to acknowledge his feelings of anger and betrayal. Having just been given a second chance at life, Peter decides to help Jinx get into "interesting trouble". Peter and Jinx rescue Camillo and Penny from Earth after Camillo's experiments with Corporate technology result in their home being besieged by the U.S. Government. Camillo and Penny are freaked out at first -- Camillo initially believes he is suffering from a psychotic break -- but they come to accept their alien benefactors. Peter shows a degree of awkwardness but a good heart, and makes a connection with Camillo and Penny after showing them some empathy, and telling Jinx to not rush off into deep space, but to land on the Moon first and make sure the humans are up for leaving home.
Key scene:
"I may never have been good at emoting, or reading others' feelings, but I think I know terror when I see it. Jinx?"
"Yeah?"
"This planet has a large sattelite. Set down on the surface, somewhere with a good view of their home. I think they need some reassurance."
"No problem... hey, Peter?"
"What?"
"You're getting better at being nice, you know that?"

Issue #2: Peter, Jinx, Camillo, and Penny explore space. Jinx takes them to an black-market space station on the edge of Corporate space run by space pirates, outcasts, and drug dealers. Penny is deeply skeptical about what she sees in space -- the absolute last thing she's expecting is English-speaking aliens trying to sell her drugs in a parking lot while a low-talent band plays three-chord buttrock. Peter is uncomfortable so close to Corporate space, and is worried that they will inevitably be followed. Jinx suggests they visit a world with known Atlantean ruins. A ruined city resembling Göbekli Tepe with an underground complex sits on broad circle of very Earth-like grass on an alien world with an oxygen-nitrogen atmosphere, a temperate climate, and abundant water, with a surface dominated by a lichen-like crust. As the humans approach the tunnel system, something seems to awaken -- tendrils of black oil reach for the humans. Peter tells them to run -- "The black oil is dangerous!" Soon they realize that Camillo and Penny need to return home after Penny almost lobotomizes herself trying to watch kung fu movies over an alien neural interface. The accident that triggers Penny's latent abilities could be a moment of conflict between the humans and the aliens, but Peter has showed enough compassion to win their trust, and Jinx takes their shock seriously and helps them understand the new situation. They decide to return to Earth.
Key scene:
Jinx rushes Penny to an improvised sickbay in one of ship's staterooms. Her examination includes both familiar elements -- eye tracking with a flashlight, etc. -- and unfamiliar ones using her psychic powers. Penny keeps repeating "I know kung fu!" which is at first treated like a symptom to some kind of neural damage. Camillo looks at an improvised EEG done with alien tech and tells Jinx, "Nah, I think it's supposed to look like that." There are no signs of seizures or other obvious abnormal electrical activity in Penny's brain. Again, Penny insists, "I know kung fu!"
Cut to the cargo hold. Jinx warns Penny that if she's wrong, she's about to get badly hurt. Penny effortlessly dodges Jinx's blows, and then catches her in an arm bar and throws her across the cargo bay. "See! I know kung fu!"
Later, Camillo, Jinx, and Peter watch Penny perform an elaborate exercise with a pair of improvised nunchaku. Camillo notices something odd, and rewinds the video feed a bit -- a nunchaku seems to clip through Penny's forearm. Jinx begins to speculate that Penny's abilities aren't necessarily purely physical...

Issue #3: We zoom in on a sleek starship, the Nightstalker, passing through a porthole into a darkened briefing room. Jolt holds two pages of hardcopy up and points savagely at it. "What the fuck is this supposed to be? You're sending us into a shoot with Arsenal and Arcana? That's not a fight, that's a squad wipe! If we're cancelled, just tell me already!" Jolt's booking agent tries to reassure her that "Honor Guard" hasn't been cancelled, despite recent low ratings, and that a newer revision is on the way as soon as the writers have had a chance to contact Jinx Bubastis. Jolt is skeptical, but gives up and changes the topic to Victor Bravo, begging her booking agent to tell his people to reassure him that she isn't trying to make him look dumb, and asks when he'll get further treatment for his condition. A few moments later, she walks out of the stateroom and composes herself. It's the ship's XO, who has been instructed to leave the ship; he gives Jolt encryption keys to take over the Nightstalker in case of an emergency. Jolt enters the bridge, and an audible warning comes that filming will begin. A scene plays out explaining that Peter and Jinx have been tracked to a pirate space station on the edge of Corporate space. Victor has trouble enunciating words like "coordinates", and Jolt makes a sincere attempt to help. Victor gets angry and snaps at her, but can't quite manage to say "interrupting". Thresher and the Eye look tense as Victor becomes more frustrated and stomps off the bridge.

Issue #3: Meanwhile, M-Seven and Honor Guard track Peter and Jinx to Earth, fight in orbit, and end up stranded on Earth. M-Seven's leader Demonslayer is superficially calm and rational, but her hatred and judgement of humanity give her a motive for her team's destructive actions -- she is confronting the invaders that destroyed her culture, and refuses to believe their protestations that they know nothing about this. Honor Guard are fish out of water, and try to ingratiate themselves with minor human authorities. Their current leader, Victor Bravo, has had a run-in with Jinx that left him with permanent brain damage -- heightened aggression and an inability to speak words of over two syllables. Media coverage of Honor Guard attracts an ambush by M-Seven. The fight between the teams ends when Tulsa, OK is destroyed by a neutron bomb. Victor Bravo panics when Honor Guard are ambushed, and deploys a neutron bomb to weaken Champion, one of M-Seven's more powerful members. He seizes the moment to beat Champion to death with a metal rod. His horrified team-mates confront Victor Bravo, who justifies his actions because "We were going to lose -- but now we win! We're the winners!" Their former leader, Jolt, relieves him of command and tries to help address the damage done, but is not up to the task and ill-equipped to assist the furious human authorities. Demonslayer gloats that "the ape primitives are about to perform an exotic ritual -- I think it's called a 'lynching'." Victor Bravo flees his team-mates, angry that they are angry at him.
Key scene:
In a farmhouse in rural Oklahoma, Demonslayer examines a plastic loaf of bread crammed full of slips of cardboard with Bible verses printed on them. She picks one and reads: "For God so loved the  world, that he gave his only begotten Son, that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life. John 3:16."
"Fascinating. Do tell me all about this God, his Son, and this John fellow. Illuminate me about your kind's starry wisdom."
The old man stammers, "It's about Jesus, the son of god, creator of heaven and earth..."
"The Creator? Really? We have psych wards full of people who think they've talked to it. You're awfully arrogant for a weak, sickly little ape thing."
Champion looks over at Demonslayer with obvious disgust. "What do you think you're accomplishing here?"
"Just asking questions. Quiet, you!"
Note: This issue introduces a ton of characters, in two super-teams. Here's a quick break-down of the current members of each team:
**Honor Guard**:
 - **Victor Bravo**: Current leader of **Honor Guard**. A heavily-armed male Fe Arran super-soldier, trying to maintain his leadership position with bluster and shouting. Knows something is wrong with him, and resents it.
 - **Jolt**: Former leader of **Honor Guard**. A striking older female Fe Arran, wears a containment suit that channels the elecricity her body generates. Tries to support Victor, which Victor sees as undermining his authority.
 - **Thresher**: A male Fe Arran cyborg with a full-body prosthesis. He has been specially adapted to move and fight in free fall. His arms feature tether projectors and retractable chainsaws. A one-man boarding party.
 - **The Eye**: A short, perky Fe Arran female acrobat and martial artist. Her eyesockets are empty and covered by mirrored lenses. She sees through an amulet fused to the top of her sternum, which gives her 360° vision and limited precognition. She fights with diamond-bladed laser knives and flashbang grenades. The chief product of her precognition is a running gag where she states the blindingly obvious in an over-dramatic fashion.
 - **Team Dynamics**: The main focus will be on the tension between **Victor Bravo** and **Jolt**. Victor resents Jolt's attempts to support him, even though she is sincerely trying to help, leaving her frustrated and resentful in turn.
**M-Seven**:
 - **Demonslayer**: An older Fe Arran female, a powerful witch/priestess dressed in dark robes and Reyll jewelry. Superficially rational, but driven by hatred for humans; she is very much a product of the culture war against the Corporation and human influence that marked the fall of the Reyll Theocracy. She was exiled from the Reyll due to mental instability, and found work in the Corporation's Fun Division as a racist caricature of the late-stage Reyll.
 - **Champion**: A tall, muscular Fe Arran male. His left eye is missing and covered with an eyepatch. He is a Superman-like flying brick with a giant copyright symbol on his chest, and is having some issues trying to live up to the currently popular "evil Superman" trope. He is more of a product of burn-out than corruption. He is a recent addition to M-Seven, finds Demonslayer's "cult leader" behavior repellent, and regrets his decision to turn "heel". Perversely, this dynamic makes him an effective Starscream to Demonslayer's Megatron.
 - **Demonslayer's Flunkies**: These two losers have been with M-Seven since the beginning, and are completely dedicated to Demonslayer's vision. **Killotron** is an android Fe Arran, allegedly with "the soul of a poet" kept in a glowing crystal on his chest. **Bearcat** is a male Fe Arran analogue to a gorilla, uplifted by Demonslayer. He is completely devoted to her and calls her "Mistress".
 - **Running Gag**: In theory, M-Seven should have seven members. In practice, Demonslayer is vile and toxic enough that they can never retain new recruits. Two recent additions abandoned the team when Demonslayer began her hunt for Peter and Jinx, **Vandal** and **The Freeze**, whho will be mentioned by name only.
 -- OLD (END)

Issue #5: Cat and Company are in hiding when they are contacted by the Magister. They've checked into a cheap motel for truckers somewhere in Illinois. He offers the aliens asylum on Earth in exchange for destroying the survivors from Honor Guard and M-Seven. Jinx's encouragement to acknowledge his negative feelings motivates Peter to accept the Magister's offer and agree to turn on his former teammates. When the humans find out about the Tulsa incident, they are strongly motivated to help their new alien friends dispose of their former teammates. Jinx is shocked by the Tulsa incident, and sees it as the consequences of her own actions; to her surprise, Peter and her human friends are supportive but offer no absolution. The Magister is very deliberately something of a cipher in this story. His motives should be unclear. Honor Guard attempts to surrender to U.S. authorities, and are ambushed and destroyed by Cat and Company.
Key scene:
Penny checks herself, Camillo, Penny, Peter, and Jinx into the motel. She explains that Peter and Jinx are on their way to a furry convention. Shortly afterwards, Jinx says, "I'll have you know I'm not **just** some kind of sex pervert, I -- who are you supposed to be?"
The Magister sits in a chair between the two double beds, watching the TV in their hotel room.
"Why hello there! I have a special, limited time offer you should **really** listen to!"
Key scene:
Peter is preparing to cremate the remains of Honor Guard in order to prevent them from falling into the hands of human authorities. He compares this to a situation after "the invasion of Magrige" where "hundreds of superheroes tried to bum-rush me." After standing his ground and killing them all, he used his powers to dig a mass grave. "I thought I'd get into trouble for making a mess." Jinx points out that he was convicted of war crimes (mass desecration of corpses) for this incident.

Issue #6: Demonslayer broadcasts a threat -- or, to be more exact, a promise -- to destroy Los Angeles. The only way to make M-Seven's attacks stop is if Cat and Company surrender Jinx's starship to the remnants of M-Seven. Victor Bravo decides to briefly pause his campaign of brutally murdering human drug dealers and purse snatchers to go out in a blaze of glory in Los Angeles. Peter and Camillo work together to defuse seven antimatter bombs Demonslayer has deployed in the L.A. basin while Jinx flies her ship to surrender it to Demonslayer. She delays Demonslayer with taunts and mockery until Camillo signals her that the bombs have been defused. While this is happening, Penny intercepts Victor Bravo in East L.A. and manages to defeat him after a running battle by stealing one of his weapons and shooting him in the chest. The signal finally comes, and Jinx takes her chance to take out the remnants of M-Seven, with considerable enthusiasm. The battle is over by the time Peter shows up to help. In the aftermath, Peter, Jinx, Camillo, and Penny will become the first public superhero team, but are unclear on what this will ultimately mean. They may be out of trouble for now, but it's quite possible their troubles are only beginning. A strange messenger appears, sent by the King in Yellow. The messenger shows the Yellow Sign tattooed on the back of her hand, and lets a translucent yellow veil fall over her face. Her posture and the structure of her face change, and the King in Yellow states that the mask is his true face and that they are speaking directly with him. He warns them that the Magister is both more and less than he seems, and invites the newly christened Cat and Company to Carcosa.
Key scene:
"Well, now, ape creatures," intones Demonslayer, "it appears we have found one of the sources of your lies. It's doomed, you should know that. Even if you give me what we want, I'm going to glass it on principle. Your choice is between my friends and I making your lives miserable for the foreseeable future, or you letting us leave in relative peace. I don't really care what you decide, it's really up to you. Choose wisely!"

Part Two: Tiamat escapes from an extradimensional prison through a portal in the middle of the Atlantic Ocean near Bermuda. She creates a monstrous super-hurricane to cause a mass casualty event in order to try to pay back her debt to the Space Bankers. The Magister's agents, The Ready Men, are unable to contain Tiamat, and by the time Cat and Company are called in, the situation threatens to become overwhelming. The Magister attempts to contact the Space Bankers, and makes an offer to purchase Earth by taking over Tiamat's debt. Peter intervenes, and directly engages Tiamat. He forces her to evert fully into ordinary space-time, and she pursues him into space. Peter finally defeats Tiamat by throwing one of Saturn's moons at her and letting her stunned body fall into Saturn, where she is torn apart by the winds of Saturn. This blind-sides the Magister, who is now on the hook for Tiamat's debt -- six billion human souls.

Part Three: Humanity may have been traumatized by recent events, but this has awakened its potential. Like an immune system reacting to a pathogen, humanity begins the process of forming a defense against the aliens among us. A woman named Sylva Ransom has long had weak but reliable precognitive abilities; a dream draws her to the World of Ash, where an entity of light from the near future calls to her. She acts like a seed crystal in a supersaturated solution. Jinx and the Magister notice the process beginning, and begin to investigate. They decide to cooperate, at least in the beginning. Once the psychic focus has been fully formed, Ransom will be able to tap into the gestalt of humanity as a whole and use its psychic potential as a kind of spirit avatar of the human race. Because of her ability to see the future, this will include humanity's future potential as well, placing her on a level comparable to Peter. The King in Yellow has also noticed, and he plans to mark Ransom with the Yellow Sign and subvert her will. The Magister's Ready Men destroy the King in Yellow by finding his undead mummified body beneath Carcosa and destroying his soul's anchor to reality. Cat and Company investigate the Magister's plans for Ransom and discover that she will be kept imprisoned in a bunker and used to harvest souls from the future to play off the Magister's debt. Horrified, they abduct Sylvia and stand watch as the locus forms, and she enters a pupal state.

Part Four: Sylvia emerges as a vastly powerful angelic being of light. Cat and Company train her in using her powers, but she is free-spirited and independent. Her powers give her a natural connection to all of humanity, but this also gives her an awareness of human suffering and injustice. She becomes a benevolent but disruptive force, and Cat and Company refuse to intervene. Sylvia pursues a vendetta against the Magister, starting by freeing the Ready Men from the Magister's mechanisms of control. When it becomes clear that she plans to expose the Magister, he makes contact with the Corporation. Before their defeat, the Reyll pursued research into connections between figures from Fe Arran legend and current figures in the Corporation, with the hope of weaponizing old "gods" against the Corporation. This research gets picked up by Corporate scientists, who see a possible explanation of Peter's powers -- and possibly, a way to reproduce them.

Part Five: Deep beneath the surface of the Earth, the Magister makes contact with the Chairman of the Board of the Corporation. He has provided the Corporate researchers with crucial data to answer the question of how Peter can survive the destruction of his body. Peter has a connection to the Big Bang that provides him with an observation point that lets him manipulate matter and energy by manipulating information -- he is a Maxwell's demon, who can arbitrarily filter flows of energy and extract work from entropy. When his body is destroyed, his consciousness retreats to this point. The Chairman informs the Magister that their attempt to create another, similar link appears to have failed.
