# **"Cat & Company" – Revised & Corrected Outline for a Comics Series**  

---

## **I. Setting & Time Period**  
- **Location**:  
  - **Earth**: Remote Catskill Mountains (Camillo’s home), corporate-controlled space stations, and alien colonies.  
  - **Carcosa**: A secretive archipelago off Portugal, ruled by **The King in Yellow**.  
  - **Space**: Corporate-controlled star cluster 400 light-years from Earth; remnants of the **Reyll Theocracy**.  

- **Time Period**:  
  - **Contemporary 21st century Earth**, with flashbacks to prehistoric civilizations and alien history.  

- **Infrastructure**:  
  - Earth’s infrastructure is **primitive** compared to alien standards, with **government suppression** of advanced technology.  
  - Corporate colonies feature **surreal, hyper-efficient architecture**; Reyll settlements blend **ancient mysticism with advanced science**.  

- **Atmosphere**:  
  - A blend of **absurdist satire**, **existential dread**, and **philosophical inquiry**.  
  - Themes of **identity, power, and the ethics of immortality** permeate the world.  
  - **Joke**: Humans are **vastly older** than the Fe Arrans, who expect humans to be far wiser and more powerful than they are.  

---

## **II. Alien Civilizations & Factions**  

### **1. The Corporation**  
- **Overview**:  
  - A **technocratic empire** born from the **Fe Arrans**, an alien race that predates humanity.  
  - Uses **cloning, conditioning, and ideological control** to dominate space.  
  - Adapts human culture for its own ends, viewing humans as **“higher beings”** to be exploited.  

- **Ideology**:  
  - **Survivalism, efficiency, and domination**.  
  - **Taboo on Earth**: Treats it as a **sacred ground** and a source of **secret knowledge**.  

- **Clones**:  
  - **Tools, not people**: Clones are stripped of autonomy and used for labor or warfare.  

- **Cultural Influence**:  
  - Corporate media features **scripted “worked” fights** between paranormals, akin to professional wrestling.  

---

### **2. The Reyll Theocracy**  
- **Overview**:  
  - An **ancient, philosophically rich civilization** that values **truth, unity, and intellectual freedom**.  
  - **Defeated by the Corporation**; now scattered remnants facing extinction.  

- **Beliefs**:  
  - Rejects gods, seeking the **truth of existence**.  
  - Considers **cloning immoral** (clones lack moral agency).  

- **History**:  
  - **Imperial Force**: Dominant among the Fe Arrans, **integrating and subsuming** other cultures.  
  - **Language as a Tool of Empire**: Conquered peoples contributed to the **lexicon**, but **grammar and writing** were codified by Reyll religious authorities.  
  - **Later Phase**: Became a **totalitarian military dictatorship** during its war with the Corporation, tearing itself apart in a **paranoid culture war**.  

- **Suppression of Paranormals**:  
  - **Strong paranormals** were labeled **mental illness**; **Rrmu Nrwwa** (a folk hero) promoted **compassion over might**.  

---

### **3. The Space Bankers**  
- **Overview**:  
  - **Ancient, inhuman entities** that view humans as **“failed investments”**.  
  - Control a **cosmic economy of souls**, using **death and afterlife as currency**.  

- **Economy of Souls**:  
  - **Necrosphere**: A **secondary ecosystem** where **Demons** act as **miners**, extracting **memories and attachments** from the dead.  
  - **Lake of Fire**: Inferno where mortal contradictions burn out; beneath lies the **World of Ash** (a surreal, empty echo of the waking world).  
  - **Fungibility**: Souls stripped of ego become **transferable**; used as **gas fees** for cosmic blockchains or reborn as new individuals.  

- **Relationship with Demons**:  
  - **Analogous to humans and yeast**: Demons spontaneously attach to complex ecosystems.  
  - **Hostile to mortals**: Demons view human morality as **self-serving delusion**.  

---

## **III. Key Locations**  
1. **Catskill Mountains (Earth)**:  
   - **Camillo Skragg’s base**; a nexus for Earth-based resistance.  
   - **Primitive but rich in hidden history** (e.g., Lemurian psychic networks).  

2. **Corporate-controlled space stations**:  
   - Centers of **technocratic control** and **paranormal exploitation**.  

3. **Carcosa**:  
   - A **closed society** ruled by **The King in Yellow**, hiding remnants of **Atlantean technology**.  
   - **Yellow Sign**: Marks individuals susceptible to possession by The King in Yellow.  

---

## **IV. Main Characters: “Cat and Company”**  

### **1. Peter Cat**  
- **Role**: A **walking weapon of mass destruction** with **energy/matter manipulation** powers.  
- **Conflict**: Struggles with **PTSD** and guilt over his violent past.  
- **Bond**: Complicated romance with **Jinx Bubastis**; seeks to support her quest for Earth’s true history.  
- **Background**: A **Cloned Series 350 Engineer** with **psychological deficits** programmed by the Corporation.  

### **2. Jinx Bubastis**  
- **Role**: A **former villain** disillusioned with nihilism; now seeks **meaning in chaos**.  
- **Abilities**: **Demonic strength, teleportation, mind-reading, and magic**.  
- **Quest**: Uncovers hidden truths tied to her **demon-fused heritage**.  
- **Anti-social tendencies**: Acts outside morality and law; a **fusion of mortal and demon**.  

### **3. Camillo Skragg**  
- **Role**: **Ex-engineer turned mental patient**; builds alien power armor to reclaim his identity.  
- **Background**: Mind-damaged by a secret project studying **Corporate technology**.  

### **4. Penny Skragg**  
- **Role**: A **slacker** with **reality-warping powers** triggered by pretending to be a martial artist.  

---

## **V. Antagonists**  

### **1. The Magister**  
- **Role**: A **scam artist** manipulating the Corporation, Space Bankers, and Reyll.  
- **Goal**: Merge Earth with the Corporation to create an **interstellar empire**.  
- **Methods**: Uses **geomantic powers, clones, and the world-mind (Akashic Records)** for control.  
- **Conflict**: Views **Cat and Company** as an **unreliable but potentially useful tool** for eliminating rivals and subordinates.  
  - **Example**: Might use them to **directly attack The King in Yellow**.  
- **Complication**: While nominally the leader of **the Illuminati**, many of his nominal subordinates have their own agendas. 

### **2. The King in Yellow**  
- **Role**: Survivor of Space Bankers’ extermination campaigns; ruler of **Carcosa**.  
- **Abilities**: Can **possess** those marked by the **Yellow Sign**.  
- **Conflict**: Hesitates to act against the Magister, fearing another **extinction event**.  
- **Complication**: Serves as a source for otherwise secret information for **Cat & Company**, who do not trust and will not work with him.

---

## **VI. Lost Civilizations & Their Legacies**  

### **1. The Lemurians**  
- **Location**: Indian Ocean.  
- **Legacy**: Created a **psychic forerunner to the Internet** (now used by the Magister).  
- **Fate**: Destroyed by the Space Bankers **75,000 years ago**.  

### **2. The Atlanteans**  
- **Location**: Strait of Gibraltar.  
- **Legacy**: Depleted Earth’s **paranormal population**; remnants in Carcosa.  
- **Fate**: Wiped out by the Space Bankers in **9600 BCE**.  
- **Connection**: **Forerunners to Fascism** and **esoteric philosophy**.  

### **3. Carcosa**  
- **Secret node** in the cosmic conflict; **hidden by the King in Yellow**.  
- **Technology**: **Advanced but medieval**; ruled by a **survivor of ancient wars**.  

---

## **VII. Human History & Anomalies**  

### **1. Humans Are Old**  
- **Millions of years** of existence; **Lemurians** integrated early hominid species into modern humans.  
- **Joke**: Humans are the **ancient precursor civilization**; the Fe Arrans expect us to be like **Vorlons or Shadows** from *Babylon 5*, but we’re **pathetically short of that**.  

### **2. Destruction of Prehistoric Civilizations**  
- **Space Bankers** destroyed early civilizations for **unpredictability**; remnants hidden in myth.  

### **3. Religion as a Paradox**  
- Human religions assume **personal gods**, leading to **pathological certainty** and **paranoid delusions**.  

---

## **VIII. Core Conflicts**  

| **Conflict** | **Details** |  
|--------------|-------------|  
| **Reyll vs. Corporation** | **Truth vs. Control** – ideological clash over humanity’s future. |  
| **Space Bankers vs. Humanity** | **Economy of Souls** – humans as “fungible” currency for cosmic blockchains. |  
| **Magister’s Manipulation** | **Scam artist** exploiting all factions to build an empire. |  
| **Magister vs. Cat and Company** | **Cat and Company** resists being a distraction; seeks **truth and autonomy**. |  
| **Cosmic Economy of Souls** | **Human souls** as “failed investments” – fate hinges on gaining legitimacy with the Space Bankers. |  

---

## **IX. Supplemental Material**  

### **A. The Economy of Souls (Parody of Cryptocurrency Scams)**  
- **Necrosphere**: A **secondary ecosystem** where **Demons** act as **miners**, extracting **memories and attachments** from the dead.  
- **Lake of Fire**: A **burning inferno** where contradictions of mortal life are purged.  
- **World of Ash**: A **surreal void** echoing the waking world; used by the **Lemurians** to access the **Akashic Records**.  
- **Fungibility**: Souls stripped of ego become **transferable**; used as **gas fees** for cosmic blockchains.  

### **B. Paranormal History**  
- **Fe Arrans**: **25%** of the population has **paranormal abilities**; **magic** is a form of **self-control**.  
- **Reyll Suppression**: **Strong paranormals** were labeled **mental illness**; **Rrmu Nrwwa** (a folk hero) promoted **compassion over might**.  
- **Corporation’s Use of Paranormals**: **“Fun Division”** scripts fights for media; **“The Hunters’ Lodge”** (Jedi-like order).  

### **C. The King in Yellow & Carcosa**  
- **The King in Yellow**: A **survivor of Space Bankers’ extermination**; **selfish and paranoid**.  
- **Carcosa**: A **hidden node** in the cosmic conflict; **Atlantean remnants** and **advanced medieval tech**.  

---

## **X. Themes & Philosophical Inquiry**  
- **Identity**: Clones vs. souls, fusion of mortal and demon (Jinx).  
- **Power**: Corporate control, Reyll’s truth-seeking, Space Bankers’ cosmic economy.  
- **Morality**: Demon cynicism vs. human delusion, the ethics of immortality.  
- **Satire**: Corporate media, magical “worked” fights, and the absurdity of the **Economy of Souls**.  

---

This outline blends **existential dread** with **absurdist humor**, offering rich material for **character arcs, worldbuilding, and cosmic-scale conflicts**. The **Economy of Souls** provides a unique lens to explore **power, identity, and the afterlife**, while **Carcosa** and **The King in Yellow** add **mythic depth**. The **Reyll’s imperial past** and **humans’ ancient superiority** over the Fe Arrans offer **rich narrative potential** for satire and philosophical inquiry.
