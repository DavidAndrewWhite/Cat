## Reference Material 

### Characters

#### **The King in Yellow** *("Malique-en-armahrl")*

- **Species**: Formerly **Human** (Atlantean). Now an immortal wizard or eldritch entity.
- **Role**: Information broker and guardian of the shattered remnants of Atlantis. Invites Cat & Company to Carcosa as guests.
- **Appearance**: Interacts with the world through humans who bear the **Yellow Sign**, the **khamalat**. Some **khamalat** are **Veil-Bearers**, whose bodies transform into an ancient, frail form with a large protruding forehead (occipital bulge), straight nose, hunched posture, and ancient hands when he is fully manifested.
- **Abilities**: Permanent possession of anyone bearing the **Yellow Sign**, significant magical knowledge, immortality, ability to transfer consciousness into alien forms over interstellar space.
- **Background**: Witnessed the destruction of Atlantis by **Exterminators** sent by the **Space Bankers**. Fled to deep space
- **Personality**: Mournful, self-deprecating about his people's hubris; somber but capable of gentle humor. Claims to have no rivals. Offers knowledge at a terrible price. Recognizes the ridiculousness of how his people operated ("Our will be done").
- **Motivation**: Wants Cat & Company to not obey the **Magister** blindly, and not expect him to tell the truth. Views them as tools to eliminate rivals (specifically the King in Yellow vs. the Magister dynamic).
- **Conflict**: Terrified of death, and willing to do anything to continue existing.

#### **The Yellow Sign**  

- Quoting Chambers:
> I opened the box. On the pink cotton inside lay a clasp of black onyx, on which was inlaid a curious symbol or letter in gold. It was neither Arabic nor Chinese, nor as I found afterwards did it belong to any human script.   
- In this interpretation, it is, in fact, Arabic -- but an ancient calligraphy of an obscure variety of Andalusian Arabic, heavily modified and stylized to make it totally non-textual. It may have started as Arabic calligraphy, but was deliberately stylized into something resembling no human script. It was built around the Arabic phrase "الشاهد الأخير" (alshaahid al'akhir) -- "The Last Witness", an esoteric eponym for **The King in Yellow** that refers to his presence just before the destruction of Atlantis.

#### Bearers of **the Yellow Sign** *("Khamalat")*

- **Role**: Humans who serve as hosts for the King in Yellow's consciousness. Selected by **the King in Yellow** as vessels to interact with the outside world.
- **Transformation**: When **the King in Yellow** fully manifests through the body of a Bearer, the body is transformed as described above.
- **Mark**: Bearers bear the **Yellow Sign** tattooed on the back of their left hand.
- **Notable individuals**:
  - **The Volunteer**: A young man sitting at the head of the dinner table in the observatory tower; lowers a veil over his face to announce the King in Yellow's approach. Healthy, recognizable as a volunteer bearer.
  - **Unnamed Young Man**: Appears with a young woman at the Steakhouse in Phoenecia; first speaks Arabic ("Salàm lacom!") and serves as the initial transformation host.
  - **Witnesses**: Carcosan officials who work in immigration control at the airport, gives **the King in Yellow** a chance to vet or respond to important visitors.

#### Jusef & Surya

- **Jusef**:
  - **Description**: A young bearded man wearing a kufi and loose clothes. Sits on a blanket with an open basket of food.
  - **Role**: Carcosan cultural guide in Camillo Skragg's dream/vision at the Atlantean archaeological site. Teaches Camillo about dream spaces, ephemeral vs. permanent text, and the "Technical Deposition of the Virus Power" (Burroughs/Gysin).
  - **Beliefs**: Follows teachings of a "wali"; understands that dreams are a kind of public space in Carcosa. Warns against permanent text in places where boundaries between worlds are weak. There is a further discussion of Carcosan religion below.
  - **Phrase**: "Salàm! I am Jusef." Uses the saying: "Jilàl di achlèm l'rizhal tamtaddu en ashir" -- "The shadows of men's thoughts lengthen in the afternoon."

- **Surya**:
  - **Description**: Jusef's 8-year-old daughter. Runs through tall red grass in Camillo's dream.
  - **Role**: Brief appearance; represents the innocence and danger of the dream-realm.

#### The Librarian

- **Role**: Resident of the observatory tower at Mushad; receives visitors on behalf of the King in Yellow.
- **Abilities**: Arranges for a khamalat (bearer of **the Yellow Sign**) to meet arriving guests; provides quarters in the tower.
- **Personality**: Formal, courteous. Speaks of the Malique-en-armahrl with respect.

#### Carcosan Corporal

- **Description**: Wears a recognizable uniform; armed only with a pistol in a holster at his belt. Stands by the white panel van.
- **Role**: Transport escort for Cat & Company from Hali to Mushad. Provides briefings en route ("famous observatory," "driver says we will be arriving shortly").
- **Significance**: Represents the Carcosan state's control over movement -- tourists cannot leave Hali city limits on their own visas; transport must be arranged.

#### Supporting Figures

- **Waitress** (Carcosa café): Warns Camillo about the Stranger's chair ("You can sit *anywhere* but in the corner -- that's the *Stranger's* place.").
- **Immigration Officer** (Hali International Airport): Directs the group to the Significant Look room; handles "unusual visas."
- **Air Hyades Staff**: Check-in agent and PA system operator.
- **The Stranger**: Implied figure associated with the empty chair at the café. Never seen, but its presence is felt as a warning.

### Locations

#### Hali

- **Description**: Capital city of Carcosa, situated on the shore of Lake Hali. Center of town has a few tall skyscrapers; most other buildings are low structures made of marble. Broad thoroughfares and plazas near the lake shore; residential areas have a few towers. Architecturally, has a significant Genoese influence.
- **Atmosphere**: Beautiful but controlled. Strict tourist visa limits prevent leaving city limits without arranged transport.
- **Key locations**:
  - **Hali International Airport**: Immigration desk with a side room featuring a plain white backdrop for the **Witnesses**.
  - **Hotel**: Near the airport; has a reception with a floor map of the Hyades archipelago.
  - **Café**: Has a chalkboard reading "You can talk about it here"; tables in the middle of the room; a forbidden Stranger's chair in the corner.

#### Lake Hali & The Hyades Archipelago

- **Layout**: Similar to the Air Hyades logo (black stars on silver). Central large island with a prominent lake containing the city of Hali marked by a large black star.
- **Islands** (west to east, northern chain):
  - **Carcosa Oscura** (Dim Carcosa)
  - **Carcosa Silente** (Silent Carcosa)
  - **Carcosa Perduta** (Lost Carcosa)
  - **Yhtill** (northernmost island, uninhabited, site of the tomb containing **the King in Yellow's** mummified body)
- **Geography**: Transition from city to country is abrupt; dirt roads follow the shoreline of Lake Hali.
- **Etymology**: The island names are based on the ones used by Genoese cartographers, who are also responsible for the name "Carcosa". **Ythill** is a distant derivative of أثل ("athel") -- a name for a [salt cedar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamarix) associated with grave sites. 

#### Mushad & The Observatory Tower

- **Mushad**: A village on the far side of Lake Hali, seemingly untouched by time. Dusk falls early; only a handful of lights. Reached by a windowless white panel van.
- **Observatory Tower**: Made of limestone; features a dome on top of a high, narrow tower. No shutter -- not used for conventional telescopes. Contains a spiral stone staircase and modest circular stone quarters lit by oil lamps. The observatory dome functions as a magic lantern projection surface.

#### Phoenecia, NY (Issue 5 closing)

- **Description**: Small, not heavily populated town in upstate New York. Center of town has small markets and bar/grills. Camillo Skragg's house is on the outskirts with a backyard landing area.
- **Role in Issue 5**: The gang's return point after the Carcosa visit; setting for the final discussion of the King in Yellow's coin and Jinx's binding.

### Carcosan Culture & Religion

#### The Yellow Sign

- **Symbolism**: Sacred symbol of the King in Yellow. Tattooed on veil-bearers (left hand) and Witnesses (right hand). Used to seal envelopes with wax. Appears painted in yellow on the side panels and rear of Carcosan transport vans.
- **Meaning**: Bearing the sign marks one as connected to the King in Yellow's power structure. Volunteers may bear the mark voluntarily; it is associated with serving as a speaking vessel.

#### Library Towers & Sacred Writing

- **Core belief**: Every village is built around a library tower where writings are kept safe. Books, ledgers, and personal journals are all considered sacred and worthy of protection.
- **Ephemeral vs. Permanent text**: Chalkboards, screens, and other temporary media are "safe." Permanent text can anchor thoughts and dreams to the real world, inviting obsession in places where boundaries between realms are weak.
- **Practical effect**: Carcosan documents (airline tickets, hotel reservations, visas) use QR codes instead of text -- digital data that can be updated without creating permanent spiritual anchors.

#### Veil-Bearers & Dream Customs

- **The veil**: A gauzy pale yellow hood used to transfer the King in Yellow's consciousness into a human host. The transformation alters the skull shape temporarily.
- **Dreams as public space**: In Carcosa, dreams are understood as a kind of shared, accessible realm. People can visit locations (like the Atlantean archaeological site) through dreams. This is not unique to Carcosa -- the colonies had visitors in dreams for a long time -- but Carcosans have developed cultural practices around it.
- **Café culture**: Establishments display chalkboards inviting dream-related conversation ("You can talk about it here"). The Stranger's chair is taboo -- avoiding it is an unspoken rule.

#### The Significant Look / "Lèkad shahidu"

- **Procedure**: Formal immigration/ceremonial process. Subject sits before a white backdrop; a Witness with the Yellow Sign tattoo instructs them to state their name while looking straight ahead. Completion is marked by "Lèkad shahidu!" ("You have been seen!").
- **Significance**: Being "seen" likely has metaphysical weight -- it may register the subject in Carcosan systems, mark them for the King in Yellow's awareness, or serve some function related to the prison around Carcosa.

#### Souls & The Lake of Fire

- **Spiritual economy**: Immortal souls are created by Tiamat and her kind as a form of currency. When a mortal dies, the soul passes through the Lake of Fire, where contradictions of self burn away. For Space Bankers, the soul becomes fungible and can be "minted."
- **King in Yellow's terror**: As an immortal being connected to this system, the King in Yellow fears that when he dies and passes through the Lake of Fire, *nothing* will remain -- his identity will be completely erased. This is the root of his desperate actions.
- **Jinx Bubastis's understanding**: She recognizes the Yellow Sign coin as a soul-urn (containing Demonslayer's remains) and understands the binding ritual's implications. Her confrontation with the King in Yellow is rooted in this knowledge.

### Science Fiction & Fantasy Elements

#### Tiamat & The Prison

- **Tiamat**: An elder being who was young when Carcosa was still cooling. True owner of Earth. Created immortal souls as currency. Tried to liquidate the human race and was fought/imprisoned by the King in Yellow and others.
- **The Prison**: A dimensional barrier built around Carcosa, visible from Penny's dream as a razor-thin white stripe defining the horizon -- like the edge of a giant dome or skull. The column of soot reaching to the top of the dome is Tiamat's imprisoned form. Penny breaks through it using her reality-warping "seven-league boots."
- **Visual characteristics**: From Penny's dream perspective: impossibly far visibility; sky tilted like the inside of a painted dome; horizon like an abyss; terrain near the edge cloudy and indistinct with tiny objects hundreds of miles away.

#### Space Bankers & Executors

- **Space Bankers**: Elder beings who view souls as currency. Made "investments" in worlds (including Earth via Tiamat). When investments failed, they sent Executors to liquidate Tiamat's creations (dinosaurs) to settle debts. View humans as a "pest control problem."
- **Executors / Exterminators**: Enforcement entities sent by the Space Bankers. Used a "gravity weapon" that transformed most of Atlantis into collapsed matter. Responsible for the destruction that forced the King in Yellow to flee.
- **Economic metaphor**: The entire cosmic history of Carcosa is framed in terms of investments, debts, liquidation, and pest control -- a dark satire of corporate/financial language applied to cosmic events.

#### Atlantis / Kisra / Carcosa History

- **Atlantis**: The name used by Greek fables (Plato). The Atlanteans had another name they "dare not speak." They were an advanced civilization that walked among the stars, built shunts throughout the galaxy, and seeded worlds with primitive humans.
- **Collapse**: A catastrophic event shattered the island nation. The King in Yellow fled as a "flash mind" to Aldebaran for millennia. The gravity weapon destroyed most of it; what remained sank to Earth's core (possibly "The Black Sun").
- **Kisra**: Name given by settlers from Al-Andalus to the ruined remnant.
- **Carcosa**: The Genoese and time transformed "Kisra" into "Carcosa."

#### The Magic Lantern Observatory

- **Description**: A planetarium-like dome of finely-cut marble with an elaborate lantern in the center. A half-dome of thin material (featureless until activated) is suspended above the lantern.
- **Function**: When lit by the King in Yellow's fingertip ignition and covered with a mantle, it projects images onto the inside of the dome -- specifically, the view from Penny's dream showing the prison and Tiamat's column of smoke.
- **Significance**: Demonstrates Carcosan technology/magic overlap; a "black box" device that uses familiar genre conventions (planetarium projection) for extraordinary purposes.

### Objects & Artifacts

#### Yellow Sign Coin

- **Description**: A gold coin bearing the Yellow Sign, standing upright in a small ring-style box. Has a strange, dark aura.
- **True nature**: Contains what's left of Demonslayer's soul -- an urn filled with the ashes of a cremated corpse. Used by the King in Yellow to attempt to bind Jinx Bubastis.
- **Binding ritual**: The King in Yellow spoke Jinx's true name ("Jnnqae Mrrlo Bebestozh") and gave an unmistakable command: "Cast no alien souls into the Lake of Fire." This is low magic Goetia, used to summon and coerce demons.

#### Air Hyades & Travel Documents

- **Air Hyades logo**: One large black star, four smaller black stars at 10 o'clock, 1 o'clock, 2 o'clock, and 11 o'clock. Stars trimmed with yellow on a silver background. Matches the layout of the Hyades archipelago map.
- **Travel documents**: Airline tickets, hotel reservations, and visas provided by the King in Yellow's emissaries contain no text -- only QR codes. This aligns with Carcosan religious beliefs about the dangers of permanent text.

#### The King in Yellow's Veil

- **Description**: A gauzy pale yellow hood or veil used to transfer consciousness into a human host.
- **Function**: When placed over a veil-bearer's head, transforms them into the King in Yellow's ancient form. The process is disorienting but non-damaging.

### Miscellaneous Notes

#### Tone & Themes for Issue 5

- **Tragedy and knowledge**: The King in Yellow offers profound knowledge at a terrible price -- his fear of oblivion drives him to manipulate others. His self-deprecation about his people's hubris makes him sympathetic despite his manipulative behavior.
- **Human madness**: The issue explores how modern humans cannot handle contact with elder realities without preparation. "I have touched the minds of modern men. Without preparation, they break."
- **Control vs. autonomy**: Multiple themes of beings trying to control others (King in Yellow binding Jinx, Space Bankers viewing all life as currency, the Magister's ambitions). Jinx's defiance is a stand for self-determination.
- **Dreams as shared space**: Carcosa treats dreams as public, navigable territory -- a fascinating blend of science fiction (altered states via neural interface) and fantasy (shared mystical realms).

#### Carcosan Language Notes

- **Carcosan** is a distant descendant of Andalusian Arabic, altered by centuries of influence from Portugal and Genoa, somewhat like the relationship between Maltese and Siculo-Arabic. While most words are based on Semitic roots, and significant elements of Arabic grammar remain, there is a significant superstratum of Iberian Romance elements. Place names are largely of Genoese origin, many words for colors are Romance, and prepositions tend towards more Romance structures.

- Phrases from the story:
  - **"Salàm lacom!"** - Greeting (Arabic-inspired, "Peace be upon you")
  - **"Lèkad shahidu!"** - "You have been seen!" (Used during the Significant Look ceremony)
  - **"Jilàl di achlèm l'rizhal tamtaddu en ashir"** - "The shadows of men's thoughts lengthen in the afternoon." (Carcosan proverb)
  - **"khamalat"** - A bearer of the veil
  - **"Malique-en-armahrl"** - The King in Yellow (title/position, not a personal name)

#### Arabic-Inspired Elements

- Issue 5 incorporates Arabic-language phrases and cultural references (kufi, Salàm greetings, wali, proverbial sayings). These reflect the historical connection to settlers from Al-Andalus who named the island "Kisra." The Arabic elements are woven into Carcosan culture as a layer of its multilingual, multi-civilization history.

#### The Stranger's Chair

- A straight-backed chair with no armrests, made of dark-stained wood, placed in the corner of the café in Hali. Associated with "The Stranger" -- an entity or concept whose nature is deliberately mysterious. The waitress explicitly warns against sitting in it. Its connection to the King in Yellow's veiled transformation (identical chair used at dinner on Page 16) suggests it may be a focal point for his presence.
